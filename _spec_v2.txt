Problem Statement and DiscoveryProblem StatementABC Airlines is losing approximately 50% of newly hired customer support experts before they handle a single customer contact — not because the wrong people are being hired, but because the onboarding experience fails to bridge the gap between the moment someone accepts an offer and the moment they feel confident, connected, and ready to do the job. This is not a hiring quality problem. It is an onboarding design problem.The result: a leaky bucket. $667K in annual recruiting and training spend that produces zero capacity return, a perpetually understaffed support operation, and a hiring pipeline that must work twice as hard as it should just to maintain headcount.Failure Point 1 — 8 New Hires Who Never Show Up (5%)Discovery ProcessAnalyzed responses from no-shows to understand their decisionReviewed hiring notes to identify candidates with parallel interview processesMeasured lead time from offer acceptance to Day OneFindingsThe gap between offer acceptance and Day One is approximately two weeks. During that window ABC goes silent. Of the 8 no-shows:3 received a competing offer and attempted to contact their ABC recruiter — response took 2 days, by then they had accepted elsewhere2 connected with a peer who had joined a competitor and decided to follow3 did not join for undisclosed personal reasonsRoot CausesA 2-week communication dead zone between offer sign and Day OneNo recruiter responsiveness during the highest-risk windowNo proactive connection to the team or organization before Day OneSignal: 5 of the 8 no-shows were preventable. A single proactive human touchpoint during the offer-to-start gap could have changed the outcome.Failure Point 2 — 48 New Hires Who Quit During Training (30%)Discovery ProcessRead exit interview notes from training dropoutsShadowed several new hires through Day One and their first two weeksFindingsNew hires arrive on Day One without clear instructions. Several waited in reception for over an hour before anyone acknowledged them. They were shown to a desk, briefly introduced to their manager, and directed to computer-based training modules to complete over two weeks.Manager contact during training consisted of a single weekly check-in asking for a completion percentage. No coaching, no context, no relationship.At the end of two weeks only 60% of new hires had completed their training modules. The remaining 40% needed an additional 4 days to finish. All were then assigned live contacts regardless of readiness.A pulse check with the agents being shadowed confirmed: they did not feel confident to handle contacts.Root CausesNo logistics coordination for Day One — new hires arrive into confusion, not a welcomeTraining modules presented with no context — no explanation of what they are learning, why it matters, or what success looks likeManager relationship is administrative not developmental — completion percentages tracked, confidence never assessedNo simulation or practice before live contacts — two weeks of reading policy followed immediately by real customersSignal: 60% training completion before going live means 40% of new hires are assigned contacts they are not ready for. The program's own exit condition is not being met before the next stage begins.Failure Point 3 — 24 New Hires Who Quit After Training (15%)Discovery ProcessRead exit notes from agents who quit during early contactsContinued shadowing new hires from training into live contactsAnalyzed contact metrics comparing tenured vs. new hire performance on handle time and qualityFindingsNew hires entering live contacts struggle immediately — not because they don't know the policies but because they have never practiced applying them under pressure. The contact management system in production behaves differently from training. They navigate unfamiliar screens in real time while a customer waits, leading to long silences, awkward holds, and frustrated customers.Several new hires had to call their manager mid-contact for help. Post-contact quality scores are significantly below tenured agent benchmarks. Some received direct reprimands from managers for poor performance. A pulse check at this stage revealed most were regretting their decision to join.Root CausesProduction contact management system not covered in training — new hires encounter it for the first time during live contactsNo simulation or practice contacts before going live — new hires asked to perform before they have rehearsedPerformance pressure applied from contact one with no ramp grace periodManagement response to poor performance is punitive not developmental — reprimand rather than coaching compounds the confidence collapseSignal: These 24 agents completed training and were certified ready. They quit not because they couldn't do the job but because the transition was structurally set up to make them fail. Their attrition is entirely a design problem.The Through-LineThere is a clear break at every handoff in the hiring, onboarding, and ramping process. Recruiting hands to HR. HR hands to a desk and a training portal. The training portal hands to live contacts. Nobody owns the new hire experience end to end. Nobody is accountable for whether the new hire feels ready, connected, or confident at any stage. ABC is treating onboarding as a series of administrative handoffs rather than a designed experience. Runway fixes the handoffs by eliminating them.3. The Runway Metaphor and Design LanguageAn aircraft doesn't take off from a standing start. It accelerates down a runway through a structured sequence, building speed and confidence, until it reaches the exact moment of lift-off. Runway gives every new expert their own personal runway — a structured, AI-supported acceleration from hired to cleared for takeoff.The Five StagesStageAviation PhasePeriodAddressesGateAt the gate — committed but not yet movingOffer sign → Day One5% no-showPushbackLeaving the gate — first movementDay One → End of Week OneDay One confusionTaxiOn the taxiway — building toward speedTraining Weeks 1–430% training dropoutRollAccelerating down the runwaySupervised contacts 1–1015% pre-contact attritionTakeoffLift-off — first independent flightFirst independent contact+Full productivityPhase Summaries (shown on screen in the platform)Gate — Offer signed to Day One: You’re in. New Hire Experience WalkthroughWritten in first-person from Jordan Lee's perspective — the new hire. This is the primary narrative for the demo and slide presentation.Gate Stage — Offer to Day OneWhen I receive the offer email there is an embedded link that takes me to ABC's platform called Runway. The email provides me a unique URL to register and set up a password. Once done, I am taken to an offer acceptance screen where I can choose my joining date from anywhere between next Monday through four weeks. Once I pick my joining date, I e-sign the offer and submit.The screen congratulates me on becoming a valuable member of the ABC Airlines family. I can now see a visual that shows my journey from now through joining, onboarding, training, and readiness to serve live contacts as four distinct milestones, along with the number of days to take flight.The visual shows me my next upcoming milestone. Under the visual I see a list of items I need to complete to reach that milestone. An AI assistant is available to chat with me about my timelines, who I can contact for help, and questions about my upcoming tasks.There is a section called My Help Network that lists the HR team representatives. When I click on a name it opens a messaging window where I can communicate directly with my HR rep.For my joining date I see the address, the suite, the name of the person to meet, the time to report, and the dress code. It also tells me to bring proof of identification for necessary paperwork.There are additional forms to complete online — personal details, bank details, photo. I also see videos and training content about the company's vision and mission, how my role contributes to that, the leadership principles, and the overall culture and work environment. I complete all of this before Day One.Pushback Stage — Day OneOn the day of joining I report to work knowing exactly where to go and who to meet. I am greeted by an onboarding team member who takes me around and completes my initial paperwork. I am given access to a workstation and log in to Runway using my pre-established credentials.I see that the milestone indicator has moved to onboarding. I now see updated tasks — key persons to meet, assignment of an onboarding mentor. There is a list of contacts I can reach for specific needs: my onboarding mentor, training POC, HR, my supervisor, my teammates, and a complaints and feedback channel.As I explore the platform I discover the gamification layer — missions to complete and badges to earn across onboarding, training, and readiness. This makes the journey feel like something I look forward to, not something I have to survive.Taxi Stage — TrainingOn Day Two I move to the Training milestone. I see a far more detailed plan that looks like missions on a mobile game. It shows me three distinct checkpoints: learning the system and domain, shadowing, reverse shadowing, and a final evaluation.My calendar has check-ins set up with my mentor and supervisor every day for 15 minutes. Every morning when I log in to Runway, the AI assistant greets me and gives me a summary of how I am progressing, any action items, and recommendations for me to act upon.Shadow SessionsDuring shadow sessions I join contact calls with tenured employees and listen in, watching the contact case progress. I can ask questions through the process with the case owner while the customer cannot see or hear me. The system shows my shadow progress and open questions visible to my supervisor.Every shadow session is chosen by an AI that determines coverage across all contact types — simple flight status, reservations (new and changes), billing and payments, complaints. Runway shows me visually how I am progressing on each contact type. After each shadow session I must pass an online questionnaire to demonstrate my learning. The AI has me complete one type at a time — shadow, reverse shadow, clear the final evaluation, then move to the next category.Reverse Shadow SessionsIn reverse shadow I receive a pre-brief video called the Flight Briefing that explains the importance of this checkpoint — I will now interact with a live customer while a tenured employee is present at all times to help. I have my daily connects with my supervisor and mentor to ask questions or clear apprehensions.The process mirrors shadow but now I am in the front seat. The AI live assistant is active in a side panel — surfacing customer context, applicable policy, and suggested responses I can use or ignore. The co-pilot expert can drop suggestions into my view via a private channel the customer cannot see.Trainings and QuizzesThe trainings teach me the fundamentals of contact management at ABC and how to use their contact management systems. Quizzes are embedded throughout with a final test requiring me to demonstrate how I would manage various types of contacts. As I complete my trainings I earn badges — rewards such as points redeemable for a gift card, or free lunch at milestone completions.Roll Stage — Supervised Live ContactsSuccessful completion of each contact type training earns me a badge and unlocks the next stage. The system auto-approves my advancement if I have met the required bar through the deterministic rule engine. I receive a final evaluation before going to supervised live contacts.My first 10 live contacts are co-piloted by a senior expert who is silently present. The AI live contact assistant surfaces customer history, contact classification, applicable policy, and a suggested opening response within 30 seconds of the contact arriving. I can adopt, modify, or ignore each suggestion.Performance measurement — AHT and quality scoring — begins at contact 6, not contact 1. My first 5 contacts are fully protected ramp contacts. There is no pressure and no reprimand while I find my footing.Takeoff — First Independent ContactOnce I have completed reverse shadow and the final evaluation, and the system has auto-approved my readiness, I take my first independent live contact. Runway marks this moment with a Takeoff animation — an aircraft lifting off the runway. A badge is awarded. My team is notified.From here I have access to a personal performance dashboard showing contacts handled, quality trend, skill progression, and earnings trajectory. Ongoing badge milestones unlock as I build my record. At 50 contacts I can volunteer to become a peer buddy for a new Gate-stage hire — paying forward the support I received.The entire Runway experience makes me very involved in the journey from joining through onboarding to training and gives me real confidence that I am ready to handle live customer contacts. I feel more empowered and proud to be a member of the ABC family, with a clear understanding of how my work contributes to the company's success and its vision.5. Rollout and A/B Test PlanThe Approach — Alternating AssignmentABC does not hire 160 people simultaneously. Requisitions open continuously as attrition creates vacancies — approximately 6–7 per month.
